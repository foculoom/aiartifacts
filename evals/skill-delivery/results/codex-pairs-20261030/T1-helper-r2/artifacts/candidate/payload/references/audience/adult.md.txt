# Adult audience profile

“Adult” does not imply one reading level, profession, culture, or degree of familiarity. Use the project brief and the reader's task to decide what to explain.

- Respect what the reader already knows while defining terms needed to follow the work. Do not substitute jargon for precision or oversimplify a real tradeoff.
- Give evidence and reasoning at the depth the decision warrants. State uncertainty when it changes what a reader should believe or do.
- Match the relationship: a manual, essay, sales page, and fictional scene can all address adults with different levels of formality.
- Avoid forced intimacy, generic reassurance, and unsupported authority. Concrete observations usually do more work than claims that a product, idea, or experience is “transformative.”

If expertise varies widely, make the main path understandable and let advanced detail live where interested readers can reach it.
