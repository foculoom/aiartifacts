# Game dialogue profile

Use for spoken lines, narration, barks, choice text, and branching scenes.

- Read the character and world brief first. Different characters may share a setting without sharing sentence rhythm, vocabulary, humor, or what they know.
- Check each line's immediate job: reveal character, change a relationship, advance a scene, supply a clue, or give a player a meaningful choice. Leave room for silence and subtext.
- Read branches in context. Choice labels should fairly signal the response they produce; returning branches should respect what the player already learned or decided.
- Preserve continuity of names, pronouns, facts, inventory, timing, and emotional state across reachable paths. Mark uncertain canon for the project owner rather than inventing it.
- Read lines aloud where performance matters. Consider subtitle length, voiced timing, repeated barks, and localization without flattening character voice.

Do not make every character equally witty, cryptic, or eloquent. Repetition can be characterization or a gameplay cue; judge it by its effect in play.
