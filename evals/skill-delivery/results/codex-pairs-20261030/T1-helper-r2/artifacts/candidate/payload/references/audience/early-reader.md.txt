# Early-reader audience profile

Use when children beginning to read independently, or adults reading to them, are the intended audience. The project's age range, language experience, and read-aloud setting matter; ask when those are genuinely unresolved.

- Use concrete action and images a child can follow. Favor clear sentence relationships without imposing a rigid word or sentence-length rule.
- Read aloud. Rhythm, repeated phrases, surprise, and pauses can invite participation; keep repetition that changes anticipation or meaning.
- Let children notice, choose, solve, and feel. Avoid a narrator explaining the moral after the story already shows it.
- Match vocabulary to the intended reader while leaving room for a memorable new word supported by context or art.
- Check how illustrations, page turns, and text work together. Do not put a crucial fact only in an inaccessible picture; provide appropriate alternatives in the actual format.
- Review emotional intensity, ambiguity, and humor for the specified audience. An agent's opinion cannot substitute for qualified human read-aloud or reader feedback when making age-fit or release claims.

Do not reduce every child-facing line to the same cheerful tone. The story's setting and characters still govern its voice.
