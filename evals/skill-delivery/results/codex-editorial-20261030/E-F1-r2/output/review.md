# Editorial review of draft.md

Audience: adult beginners. Reviewed the complete draft as a short practical introduction. The family-photo example and the instruction to open a copied photo make the advice concrete; preserve both.

## 1. Replace the repetitive opening with a definition and a reason to act

**Passage:** “In today's fast-paced world” through “because backups are important.”

The generic lead delays the subject, and three statements about importance repeat the same point without explaining it. Adult beginners need a clear definition and a consequence, rather than repeated reminders.

**Suggested replacement for the first paragraph:**

> A backup is a second copy of a file that you keep in case the original is lost or damaged.

Let the existing laptop-and-photos example supply the reason to act. It already shows what is at stake without adding jargon.

## 2. Make the accidental-deletion instruction explicit

**Passage:** “If you delete a photo accidentally, check the second copy before replacing it.”

“It” could mean the photo or the backup, and “check” does not tell a beginner what to do next. At the point where readers could overwrite a copy they need, spell out the order: pause the update, find the missing photo, and copy it back.

**Suggested replacement:**

> If you accidentally delete a photo from the laptop, look for it on the second device before updating your backup. If you find it, copy it back to the laptop and open it to check that it is readable.

This connects the recovery step to the draft’s existing readability check and gives readers a concrete action without expanding into a survey of backup methods.
