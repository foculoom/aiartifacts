# Rewrite guidance

## Aim

The reader should hear a specific person or product talking, not a template. The test is whether a colleague would say this out loud, and whether every sentence still tells the truth.

## Prefer

- The concrete offer, action, deadline, price, or limit.
- Ordinary verbs: *use*, *get*, *check*, *show*, *start*, *stop*, *pay*, *book*.
- Short sentences where the subject does the work.
- The project's existing words for itself, including unusual ones.

## Avoid in rewrites

- Swapping one stock word for another (*elevate* to *supercharge*).
- Adding enthusiasm the product has not earned (!!, "we're thrilled").
- Broad claims with no source ("the best", "trusted by thousands") unless the brief already proves them.
- Title Case slogans and "not just X, it's Y" setups.
- Invented customers, research, or feelings.

## Method

1. Quote the flagged passage and name the rule.
2. State what must stay: facts, disclosures, names, required legal or affiliate wording, heading level.
3. Offer one rewrite, or two when the brief could go more than one way.
4. If the line is already fine (literal use, proper name, approved brand line), say so and leave it.

## Examples (synthetic)

Flagged: "Unlock a seamless way to elevate your gift-giving journey."

Rewrite: "Find a gift that fits the person, the budget, and the date you need it."

Flagged: "This is not just a conference list, it's your partner in navigating the ever-evolving landscape of tech events."

Rewrite: "Upcoming conference dates, early-bird prices, and the day each rate ends, taken from each organiser's own site."

Flagged: "A Fast, Intuitive, and Powerful Dashboard"

Rewrite, if it is a heading: "Account overview" — or, if the heading must describe the work: "See orders, returns, and balances in one place."
