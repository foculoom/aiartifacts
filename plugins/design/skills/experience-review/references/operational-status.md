# Operational status and private operations

Review the public status page and private operations view as separate journeys.
Record what each probe actually exercises, its cadence, confirmation rule and
last trustworthy observation. A reachable game entry page does not prove every
game action works; opening a hosted form does not prove submissions arrive.
Compare green headlines, badges and incident titles with that exact scope.
Keep products still in development outside live uptime totals.

Exercise initial loading, healthy, first failure, confirmed disruption, first
recovery, confirmed recovery, stale monitoring and provider/API failure with
safe fixtures. A successful complete read with no incidents can say none were
recorded; a failed or incomplete read must say history is unavailable.
Known services should remain identifiable when their current state is unknown.
Check that incident records and state transitions cannot diverge after a
partial write, and that feeds do not present stale ongoing claims as current.

For every private metric, identify source, unit, reporting window, last refresh,
sampling or estimation, and known QA or duplicate limits. Suppress or clearly
mark a cached number when its refresh is stale. Page loads and form submissions
are not people. A public response should expose an explicit allowed shape even
if its storage row later gains private fields.

Inspect anonymous and authorized paths separately. A screenshot of a private
dashboard does not prove its access policy; source configuration alone does
not prove a founder login works. Record which authentication, narrow-screen,
keyboard and failure journeys were actually exercised. Do not submit real
forms, create incidents, change credentials or expand telemetry merely to
complete an audit.
